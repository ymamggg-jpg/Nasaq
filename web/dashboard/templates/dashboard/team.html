About us

A team of two students in the web development bootcamp at Tuwaiq Academy.

We built Nasaq for the Expo 2030 Riyadh challenge at Tuwaiq Academy, track 3 (Operations, Safety and Security), challenge 3.1: helping the site operations team detect congestion early enough to act.

Why we built it

When thousands of visitors move at the same time, a count of who is at a gate right now arrives too late. We wanted a tool that warns before the crowd forms, and a safe place to try a decision before applying it to real people.

We came from web development, with no prior experience in machine learning or crowd simulation. We learned both while building this project.

How we work

The rules we followed for every result on this site.

  1. Settings first, results second

    We wrote down each test's settings before running it, and did not change them after seeing the result.

  2. Failures are shown

    When the model lost to a simple method, the page says so.

  3. Every number from a file

    The result numbers on this site are read from our result files, not typed by hand.

  4. Simulated or real, always labelled

    Each result says whether it comes from our simulator or from real data.

  5. Assumptions in the open

    Every value we did not measure is listed as an assumption on the console page.

What this site is, and is not

  • A competition prototype, not an official tool of Expo 2030 Riyadh or any authority.
  • It runs on a small test layout, not on the real Expo site, and is not connected to live data.
  • It collects no data about its visitors. Your choices (language, theme, scenario) stay in your own browser; fonts load from Google Fonts.
