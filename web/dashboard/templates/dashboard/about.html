Rehearse the crowd before the gates open.

Nasaq simulates visitors at an Expo gate, forecasts the crowd at each door 10 seconds ahead, and lets an operations team test a decision, such as closing a route, before applying it.

Open the console See what we found

Built and tested inside a crowd simulator. Not connected to live Expo data.

The problem

“The site operations team struggles to detect congestion early enough to act, when thousands of visitors move between gates, sites, transport points and public spaces at the same time.”

Challenge 3.1, Operations, Safety and Security track

A count of who is at a door right now arrives too late: by the time a door looks full, the queue behind it is already forming. Nasaq looks ahead instead, and gives the team a safe place to try a decision first.

What we found

All results come from our own simulation runs on a test layout. Each is the mean over several runs the model never trained on.

    What it is built with

    JuPedSim 1.4.2
    Open pedestrian simulator from Forschungszentrum Jülich. Moves every visitor with the Collision-Free Speed Model.
    scikit-learn
    Gradient-boosting model that forecasts the crowd at each door 10 seconds ahead.
    pandas
    Turns recorded runs into training data and results tables.
    Django · JavaScript
    This site. Every run is precomputed, so the console needs no live simulation.
    Seyfried et al. 2007
    A real bottleneck experiment from Jülich, used to check the simulator.

    Honest limits

    • The layout is a test layout (two rooms, two doors, one exit), not the Expo site.
    • The model is trained on our own simulations, so results show the method works inside the simulator, not on real crowds.
    • Door capacity in the routing rule is an assumption (1.3 persons/s per metre); we tried 1.0 and 1.6 and the conclusion did not change.
    • We do not model fire or smoke, and this is not a certified evacuation plan.
    • Response time, steward compliance, density limits and visitor levels are our assumptions.

    What comes next

    • Feed the forecast with real gate counters and sensors.
    • Model the real site layout once an operational map is available.
    • Train the gate warning on recorded crowd data, not only on simulations.
    • Calibrate the simulator to the measured real flow, then repeat the tests.
